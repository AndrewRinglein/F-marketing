Four practical improvements.

01

Put a text signup on the bingo page

Invite visitors to receive Tuesday reminders and current hot-ball news. I did not see an SMS signup on the page I reviewed. Include the buying link so a reminder can turn directly into a planned visit.

First step: put the signup or offer beside the session players are choosing.

02

Presell the $61 buy-in

Show what the pack includes, take the order ahead of time and send a clear pickup receipt. Give advance buyers a separate pickup point while keeping paper bingo exactly as familiar as it is today.

First step: choose one easy benefit and explain how players earn it.

03

Start a Scholarship Players Club

Let guests enroll once and earn a simple thank-you for repeat visits. A snack-bar perk and a birthday greeting can give regulars another reason to choose your Tuesday.

First step: try this with one session and compare attendance and staff time.

04

Promote the hot ball you already run

Put its current amount in large type, next to the date and pack price. Add a short story about the scholarships bingo supports, then share that same clear invitation with players and local supporters.

First step: give one person ownership and review the results each week.

Staffing and game details.

You may be able to pay staff using funds your charity raises outside bingo. We can help organize the operational records while your adviser checks the funding arrangement and local requirements.

Read the supporting guidance

Staffing: review the source of funds and the role.

California Penal Code §326.5(b) and (h) restrict wages from bingo and require member operation, with a security-personnel exception. Genuinely separate, non-bingo funds may be an avenue for counsel to assess; they are not blanket permission to compensate bingo workers. Read the current statute.

In Live and Learn v. City of Los Angeles (1986), the court rejected paying employees with bingo proceeds even for non-bingo charitable work. That ruling does not itself approve every outside-funded staffing arrangement. Ask counsel to review duties, membership, funding restrictions, labor rules, and local permit conditions. Document separate accounts and time records; relabeling bingo receipts does not change their source. Read the decision.

A reported example to investigate: Redwood City Pulse reported in September 2023 that Vanguard paid bingo workers from non-bingo donations and that its attorney described Santa Clara police-chief approval. This is a concrete arrangement for counsel to investigate. It is a reported local practice, not a statewide court ruling or a verified approval for your hall. Request the underlying arrangement and local written guidance before adopting it. Read the reported example.

Hot ball: the exact game matters.

The current §326.5(n) limit is $500 in total prizes per separate game. The Attorney General’s 1999 power-ball opinion rejected a bonus pool that exceeded the then-$250 limit. A separate pool did not avoid the cap. Review base prize plus bonus, funding, carryovers, and local rules before advertising a hot ball. This is an option to evaluate, not a pre-approved game. Read Opinion 98-1202.

Must-go: publish the exact award conditions.

A must-go label describes an award commitment; it does not create a separate exemption from prize or permit rules. Specify the game, session, amount, qualification, pattern, tie handling, and conditions for awarding the prize. Review the total payout and funding under the applicable rules. A historic drawing advertisement is not proof of approval for a new bingo format.

Text messages: permission first, measurement second.

Use an explicit, optional marketing opt-in with the hall’s identity, expected frequency, message/data-rate notice, terms and privacy links, and a clear opt-out. Retain consent evidence; honor STOP and other reasonable revocations. Check current provider and legal requirements, including whether the provider permits this bingo campaign. Never buy a phone list or treat a purchase as consent. FCC guidance.

These notes flag review items for California charitable bingo. Local authorities regulate these games; they are not a legal opinion or tribal-gaming guidance. California Gambling Control Commission.
